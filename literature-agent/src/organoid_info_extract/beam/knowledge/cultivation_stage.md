# CULTIVATION STAGE DEFINITIONS
For each culture step, test categories in the following order: Seeding → Patterning → Differentiation → Expansion → Self_Organization → Maturation → Maintenance. As soon as a step clearly matches one category, assign that category and STOP; do NOT attempt multi-labeling. For example, a step that both changes medium composition and induces lineage commitment should be labeled as Differentiation if it meets Differentiation criteria, even if it also results in proliferation. A step that introduces regional cues and also supports growth should be labeled as Patterning if patterning cues are explicit. This priority rule must be applied deterministically for every step.

## Culture System Types
- Scaffold-based Systems: Cultures utilizing natural or synthetic 3D matrices (e.g., Matrigel, PEG hydrogel) to provide structural and biochemical support for cell growth and organization.
- Suspension-based Systems: Cultures where cells or cell aggregates are maintained in low-adhesion or dynamic suspension environments, promoting spontaneous aggregation and self-organization.
- Interface-based Systems: Cultures grown on porous membrane supports (e.g., Transwell inserts) that allow access to medium from both apical and basal sides, often used for epithelial tissues.
- Engineered Microenvironments: Cultures established within microfluidic devices, chambers, or droplet-based systems that provide precise control over the cellular microenvironment.
- Bioprinting & Assembly: Techniques involving the layer-by-layer deposition of cell-laden bioinks or the assembly of microtissues into predefined architectures.
- Bioreactor Systems: Perfusion-based culture systems that provide dynamic nutrient flow and waste removal, supporting large-scale tissue growth and maturation.

## Category Definitions
### 1.Category: Seeding
- Core Concept: The initial introduction of cells into the culture setup.
- Unified Definition (Default): Initial loading of cells into a defined culture environment to initiate aggregation, attachment, or spatial organization.
- Context-Specific Criteria:
  - Scaffold-based Systems: Cells are introduced into or onto natural or synthetic 3D matrices (e.g., Matrigel, PEG hydrogel, alginate, PLGA scaffolds).
  - Suspension-based Systems: Cells are transferred into low-adhesion or dynamic suspension environments to promote spontaneous aggregation.
  - Interface-based Systems: Cells are deposited onto porous membrane supports such as Transwell inserts.
  - Engineered Microenvironments: Cells are precisely injected into microfluidic channels, chambers, or droplet-based compartments.
  - Bioprinting & Assembly: Cell-laden bioinks are deposited layer-by-layer or microtissues are positioned into predefined architectures.
  - Bioreactor Systems: Pre-formed immature organoids or microtissues are loaded into a perfusion chamber.

### 2.Category: Expansion
- Core Concept: The phase of increasing cell number, tissue mass, or density.
- Unified Definition (Default): Promotion of rapid proliferation of stem/progenitor cells under growth factor-rich conditions to increase tissue mass and cellular density.
- Context-Specific Criteria: 
  - Scaffold-based Systems: Cells proliferate within the structural and biochemical support of 3D matrices.
  - Suspension-based Systems: Aggregates expand in scaffold-free environments supported by nutrient-rich, suspension-compatible media.
  - Interface-based Systems: Cells proliferate and form stratified layers under submerged conditions.
  - Engineered Microenvironments: Cells expand within confined microfluidic spaces supported by perfused nutrient flow.
  - Bioprinting & Assembly: Printed constructs or assembled microtissues proliferate within biocompatible matrices and perfusable scaffolds.
  - Bioreactor Systems: Perfused bioreactors enable large-scale tissue expansion through continuous nutrient delivery and waste removal.

### 3.Category: Patterning
- Core Concept: The establishment of spatial organization, regional identity, or polarity.
- Unified Definition (Default): Induction of region-specific identities and developmental axes through biochemical cues or physical microenvironmental signals.
- Context-Specific Criteria:
  - Scaffold-based Systems: Growth factors and scaffold-associated mechanical or biochemical cues guide regional fate specification.
  - Suspension-based Systems: External morphogens pattern free-floating cell aggregates to establish polarity and regionalization.
  - Interface-based Systems: Basal-side signals instruct polarity and layer-specific organization in membrane-supported cultures.
  - Engineered Microenvironments: Spatial patterning is engineered through gradients, shear forces, or multi-material 3D printing.
  - Bioprinting & Assembly: Digital design governs spatial placement of cell types and bioactive factors during printing.
  - Bioreactor Systems: Patterning is established before loading and is maintained or refined under perfusion.

### 4.Category: Differentiation
- Core Concept: The transition from progenitor states to specific lineage commitments.
- Unified Definition (Default): Transition from progenitor expansion to lineage commitment by withdrawing proliferative cues and introducing differentiation signals.
- Context-Specific Criteria:
  - Scaffold-based Systems: Organoids differentiate within supportive ECM-like scaffolds.
  - Suspension-based Systems: Patterned aggregates differentiate into terminal cell types in suspension culture.
  - Interface-based Systems: Air-liquid interface strongly enhances terminal differentiation in epithelial tissues.
  - Engineered Microenvironments: Differentiation is driven by perfused medium and local physicochemical microenvironment.
  - Bioprinting & Assembly: Spatially organized constructs differentiate based on embedded cues and controlled medium composition.
  - Bioreactor Systems: Differentiation may continue within the perfused system depending on media composition.

### 5.Category: Maturation
- Core Concept: The long-term acquisition of advanced structural and functional features.
- Unified Definition (Default): Long-term culture-driven acquisition of advanced structural, metabolic, and functional features characteristic of mature tissues.
- Context-Specific Criteria:
  - Scaffold-based Systems: Extended culture enhances ECM deposition and complex internal architecture.
  - Suspension-based Systems: Dynamic suspension systems improve nutrient exchange and functional maturation.
  - Interface-based Systems: Air-liquid interface enables full epithelial barrier development and mature phenotypes.
  - Engineered Microenvironments: Continuous perfusion, mechanical forces, and engineered architecture enhance physiological maturation.
  - Bioprinting & Assembly: Perfusion bioreactors or long-term culture promote functional reinforcement of printed constructs.
  - Bioreactor Systems: Sustained perfusion and mechanical stimuli drive high-level tissue maturation.

### 6.Category: Self_Organization
- Core Concept: Intrinsic, cell-driven formation of structures (lumens, layers, etc.).
- Unified Definition (Default): Intrinsic cell-driven organization into polarized, lumen-forming, or multi-layered structures shaped by local cues and developmental programs.
- Context-Specific Criteria:
  - Scaffold-based Systems: Self-organization is modulated by scaffold geometry and biochemical composition.
  - Suspension-based Systems: Aggregates autonomously form lumens, polarity, and layered architectures without external scaffolds.
  - Interface-based Systems: Self-organization is minimal; morphology is largely dictated by externally imposed physical constraints.
  - Engineered Microenvironments: Cell autonomy is constrained by engineered boundaries, resulting in limited large-scale self-organization.
  - Bioprinting & Assembly: Cells refine the printed architecture through local rearrangement and network formation.
  - Bioreactor Systems: Major self-organization occurs before loading; the bioreactor primarily maintains outcomes.

### 7.Category: Maintenance
- Core Concept: Ongoing support of established structures and functions without major changes.
- Unified Definition (Default): Sustained culture conditions that preserve existing tissue architecture, cell viability, and functional properties without inducing significant growth, differentiation, or reorganization.
- Context-Specific Criteria:
  - Scaffold-based Systems: Continued culture within stable scaffolds that maintain tissue integrity and function.
  - Suspension-based Systems: Ongoing suspension culture that preserves aggregate structure and viability.
  - Interface-based Systems: Sustained air-liquid interface conditions that uphold epithelial barrier function.
  - Engineered Microenvironments: Stable perfusion and environmental control that maintain established tissue states.
  - Bioprinting & Assembly: Long-term culture of printed constructs under conditions that preserve architecture and function.
  - Bioreactor Systems: Continuous perfusion and environmental regulation that sustain mature tissue characteristics.

### 8.Category: Others
- Core Concept: Specialized procedures falling outside standard development stages, including external treatments/perturbations.

2.Match Criteria: Check the Context-Specific Criteria for that system.
3.Fallback: If the system is unspecified or the text implies a general concept, use the Unified Definition.
